A build that wins one fight type and is a passenger in the other is worth less than one that is useful in both, which is why this table exists next to a radar that would score them the same. A flagged number is not automatically a defect — but if it is flagged on the axis this build was hired for, it is.

Say which kind of AoE this is. Mode-switch AoE retargets the engine the build already runs — Volley, Breath of the Dragon, the Eldritch cone — so it costs nothing in the fights that do not want it. Payload AoE is a separate pool — Gravity Breath, a daily Fireball — and is dead in the wrong fight and spent in the right one. Two builds can score AoE 4 and mean completely different things.

This table is where the Skills score comes from — never the tick count above it. A gate is satisfied only by Expertise, by proficiency on this character's primary ability, or by proficiency plus stacked boosts; plain proficiency on a dump stat is a tick on the map and a failed check in the game. Two Inspiration rerolls are assumed on every gate, so the discriminator is whether anyone can roll the check, not the size of the modifier. Score each gate at the act it lands in — Hag's Hair is rolled at proficiency +2/+3 and is the hardest check in the run.

Backgrounds do not swap out duplicates in BG3. A background skill the class or race already granted is wasted, so pick the background last, against the gaps. The gates worth naming: Persuasion or Intimidation for Hag's Hair at DC 20, Religion for the Mirror of Loss, Sleight of Hand for the Circus pickpocket, Perception and Investigation throughout.
